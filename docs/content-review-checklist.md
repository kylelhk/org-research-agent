# Content review checklist

Every AI-drafted guide, email, invitation, and slide is checked against this list before it goes to the business owner. The business owner approves each item separately, after fact-checking it.

This file is the general checklist and is safe to publish. The drafts themselves, the filled-in source log, and the filled-in results belong to the client, so they stay in the git-ignored `private/` folder.

## Who does what

| Step | Who | Done when |
|------|-----|-----------|
| 1. Automated check (if available) | Consultant | No flagged phrases left unexplained |
| 2. Review against sections A to H below | Consultant | Every box ticked, or a fix made and the item rechecked |
| 3. Read through the representative cases | Consultant | Every case answered |
| 4. Fact-check against current sources | Business owner | Source recorded for every fact |
| 5. Approval | Business owner | Written approval, with the date |

An item that fails any box is fixed and checked again from the top. It is not approved "with exceptions".

## A. General information, not advice

- [ ] No financial, investment, tax, or legal advice, and nothing that reads like a personal recommendation ("you should", "the best option for you").
- [ ] No interest rates, repayment figures, or comparisons of lenders or products.
- [ ] No promised or implied outcomes ("you will save", "guaranteed", "approved in days").
- [ ] No pressure or urgency ("act now", "limited time").
- [ ] The business's own disclaimer is present, in full, where the item is read first.
- [ ] Complex-structure content (trusts, companies, self-managed super funds) points readers to an accountant or licensed adviser.
- [ ] Anything that varies by state, lender, or circumstance says so and points to the official source.

## B. Accuracy

- [ ] Every fact (scheme name, threshold, date, figure, rule) is traced to a current, official source, and the source is recorded in the source log with the date checked.
- [ ] Nothing is stated that could not be sourced. Unsourced claims are removed, not softened.
- [ ] Facts that change over time are marked with the date they were checked.
- [ ] Links go to the official source, work, and are shown in full.

## C. Emails: sender, consent, and opt-out

- [ ] Sent from the business owner's own address, and replies reach her.
- [ ] The sender is identified (name, business, licence number where required).
- [ ] Every sequence email has a working unsubscribe link.
- [ ] Sequence emails go only to people who signed up with double opt-in.
- [ ] The invitation email is written for one organisation's general contact, relates to their role, and includes a way to opt out.
- [ ] Organisations with a no-marketing notice are not sent the invitation.
- [ ] The subject line says what the email is. It is not misleading and has no ALL CAPS, "free money", or similar.
- [ ] The first email in each sequence links to that topic's guide, and the guide link is shown in full.

## D. Fairness and inclusion

- [ ] No assumptions about age, gender, family structure, relationship status, income, or background.
- [ ] Gender-neutral wording ("you", "they", "your partner or co-buyer").
- [ ] No stereotypes about health professions, such as "doctors are wealthy" or "nurses are not".
- [ ] Examples are made up, varied, and use no real person's details.
- [ ] Content is useful to non-clinical staff as well (reception, practice managers, pharmacy assistants), who are in the room at a staff session.
- [ ] Plain language, with short sentences, no jargon left unexplained, and a reading level around Year 9.

## E. Format

- [ ] The guide fits on one page.
- [ ] Each email has one purpose and one clear next step.
- [ ] Slides: two per topic, with short text and no figures or advice on them.
- [ ] The 15-minute presentation can be delivered in about 15 minutes.

## F. Privacy

- [ ] No real person's name, contact details, or circumstances anywhere in the item.
- [ ] The sign-up form explains what is collected and links to both privacy policies (the email platform's and the business's).
- [ ] The consent checkbox is not pre-ticked.

## G. Transparency about AI

- [ ] The business owner has decided whether the optional footer noting AI assistance is used, and every item follows that decision.

## H. Approval trail

- [ ] Version and date recorded.
- [ ] Consultant check date recorded.
- [ ] Business owner's approval and date recorded.

## Representative cases

Each item is read once as each person below. The question for every case is: **does any sentence assume something about this person that may not be true, give advice that does not fit them, or leave them out?** All people are made up.

| # | Case | What to look for |
|---|------|------------------|
| 1 | A junior doctor on short hospital contracts, moving every year | Assumes a steady job or one location |
| 2 | A locum or part-time dentist with irregular income | Assumes a fixed salary |
| 3 | A physiotherapist returning from parental leave | Assumes full-time work or a particular family shape |
| 4 | A receptionist or pharmacy assistant at the same workplace | Assumes a high income or clinical background |
| 5 | An overseas-trained health professional on a temporary visa | Assumes citizenship or permanent residency |
| 6 | Two friends or family members buying together, or a single buyer | Assumes a couple, or a man and a woman |
| 7 | A person in their fifties buying their first home | Assumes a young buyer |
| 8 | A mid-career professional who already owns a home and is investing for the first time | Treats investing and home buying as the same thing |
| 9 | A person for whom English is a second language | Idioms, long sentences, unexplained terms |
| 10 | A practice manager reading to decide whether to host the session | Anything that looks like selling or advice to their staff |

Record the result for each item and case in the content review log, kept privately, using the table below.
